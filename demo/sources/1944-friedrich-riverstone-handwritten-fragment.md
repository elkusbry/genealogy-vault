# 1944 V-mail fragment — Friedrich Riverstone, Normandy

## Description

The lower third of a V-mail postcard from Friedrich to Klara, dated
July 29, 1944 — 11 days before he was killed. The upper portion is
torn off; what survives is the closing paragraph and signature.
Handwriting is rushed pencil cursive, faded but legible.

This is the only surviving piece of Friedrich's wartime
correspondence in this vault.

## Cleaned Text

> ...the food is no worse than the shop's coffee, Ma. We move tomorrow
> — I can't say where. Tell Pop the boys here are good ones, mostly
> kids from Ohio and Pennsylvania, and they joke about my accent which
> I didn't know I had. I'll write again when we stop moving.
>
> Love to all,
> Freddy

## Extracted Facts

- **1944-07-29** — Friedrich's unit was about to move; he was alive
  and uninjured.
- He had an audible accent that fellow soldiers noticed — likely
  trace German from the bilingual Riverstone household.
- He was killed 11 days later, August 9, 1944, near Saint-Lô.
- His mother went by "Ma," father by "Pop," consistent with other
  family correspondence.

## Notes

The torn-off upper portion (containing the date stamp, censor's mark,
and opening) was presumably destroyed by Klara — she kept letters as
mementos but cut off any portion that referenced specific locations or
unit movements, a wartime habit she described to Mary.

The unit ("29th Infantry Division") is corroborated by Friedrich's
person file but not stated here.
