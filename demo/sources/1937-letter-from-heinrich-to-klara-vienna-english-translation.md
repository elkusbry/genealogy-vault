# English translation — 1937 letter from Heinrich to Klara (Vienna)

**Translator's note.** Heinrich's prewar German is straightforward —
formal but not literary — except in the second paragraph, where he
slips into a more idiomatic, almost wistful register when speaking of
Vienna. I've tried to preserve that shift in the English. "Werkstatt"
is rendered "shop" rather than "workshop" because that's what the
family called the Philadelphia store.

## Translation

> Vienna, August 14, 1937
>
> My dearest Klara,
>
> Karl is weaker than I had thought. The doctor spoke with me this
> morning — his heart won't hold out much longer. I will stay another
> week, perhaps ten days. Please write to Mary and tell her I cannot
> bring her birthday present in person this year.
>
> The city is no longer the city of our youth, Klara. The swastikas in
> the windows, the uniforms on every street. Karl says it will get
> worse, not better. We talk about the children, about Philadelphia,
> about the shop. Sometimes I forget that I have not been here in
> twenty-five years.
>
> Kiss the children for me. Tell Friedrich to take good care of the
> shop while I am away.
>
> Yours,
> Heinrich

## Translation Notes

- **"Die Hakenkreuze in den Fenstern"** — literally "the hooked-crosses
  in the windows." I've used the now-conventional English "swastikas."
  Heinrich's term is the neutral compound, not the loaded loanword.
- **"Werkstatt"** — workshop, but I've used "shop" because that's
  how the family referred to **Riverstone Furniture & Cabinetry**.
- **"Sich gut um etwas kümmern"** — to take good care of something /
  to look after. The English doesn't quite capture the gentle
  paternal tone; Heinrich is asking, not ordering.
- **Date format**: German "am 14. August 1937" rendered as "August 14,
  1937" per US convention for this vault.
